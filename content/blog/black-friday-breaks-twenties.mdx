It is 6:10 on Black Friday morning. A customer in a work jacket sets a $4 coffee on your counter and slides a hundred-dollar bill across it like a closing argument. Your cashier opens the drawer. There are two fives in it.

That is not a customer service problem. It is an inventory problem, and the inventory is money.

Every convenience store runs two businesses under one roof. The one everyone sees sells fuel, coffee, snacks and lottery tickets. The other one is quieter, and it sells change. It takes big bills from customers and hands back small ones, all day long, at a margin of exactly zero. Most weeks nobody notices that second business exists. On Black Friday weekend, it is the busiest department in the building.

## The Drawer Runs Out Before the Day Does

Holiday traffic does something specific to cash. Customers walk in with larger bills for gifts, travel and parking, then spend them on small things along the way. The twenties, fifties and hundreds come in across the counter. The fives and ones go out. By midmorning the drawer looks like a wallet after a weekend in Las Vegas: heavy at one end, empty at the other.

The fix most stores reach for is the oldest one. Somebody grabs a bank bag, leaves the store, drives to the branch, waits in line and comes back with straps and rolls. On a slow Tuesday that is an inconvenience. On one of the busiest shopping weekends of the year, it means a manager is off the floor during peak traffic, carrying cash through a parking lot at exactly the hour the store needs them most.

And if the branch is closed or the line is long, the cashier starts improvising. Asking customers for smaller bills. Sending people next door for change. Making a sale wait while someone digs through the safe. Each workaround costs a little time and a little goodwill, and Black Friday has none of either to spare.

## Meanwhile, the Safe Fills Up

The other half of the problem is quieter. All those big bills have to go somewhere. Registers get pulled, drops go into the safe, and the back office slowly fills with the best revenue of the quarter sitting in a steel box nobody has time to count.

Deposits wait for a slow moment that never arrives. The cash sits overnight, then over the weekend, and the money from the biggest days of the season reaches the bank after the season has already moved on. Last week we made the case that [cash sitting in a machine is money that is not working for you](/resources/blog/your-cash-is-sitting-in-the-machine). Cash sitting in a back-office safe is the same problem with worse lighting.

So the holiday weekend squeezes the store from both ends. Not enough small bills up front. Too many large ones in the back. And the person who is supposed to fix both is behind the counter, because it is Black Friday.
