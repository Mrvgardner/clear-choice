Every ATM you own is also a small, very secure savings account that pays you nothing.

It sits in the corner of a convenience store, holds a few thousand dollars of your money, and hands it out twenty at a time to strangers. Then it waits for you to fill it again. You never applied for this account. You opened it the day you bought your first terminal, and you have been making deposits ever since.

Most operators stop seeing it. The cash in the cassette becomes part of the furniture, a cost of doing business, the price of admission. That is a mistake in any month. In December, it gets expensive.

## The Money Nobody Counts

Run the math on your own fleet. Take a modest example: twenty terminals, each loaded with eight thousand dollars. That is $160,000 of your capital standing in line behind a cassette door. Add the float you keep for the next load and the buffer for the machine that always runs dry on Saturday, and the real number climbs.

None of that money is lost, which is exactly what makes it easy to ignore. It comes back twenty dollars at a time through settlement. But while it sits in the machine, it is not buying a new terminal, funding a placement, covering a slow month, or doing anything else a dollar in a growing business should be doing.

There is also the cost that never shows up on a spreadsheet. Somebody has to pull that cash from the bank, count it, carry it, and load it. If that somebody is you, the price is your time and your attention. If it is an employee, it is payroll and a level of trust you have to keep earning. Either way, the cash in your terminals has a handler, and the handler is on your clock.

So ask a blunt question. If a lender handed you $160,000 tomorrow, would you lock it in twenty steel boxes and walk away? That is the deal most operators have quietly made with themselves, and they made it without anyone offering better terms.

## December Raises the Stakes

Holiday cash demand does not arrive politely. Shoppers pull more cash, travelers pull more cash, and every seasonal location you signed this fall wants a full cassette on the busiest weekend of the year. We wrote in September about why [Q4 placements close before October](/resources/blog/q4-placements-close-in-september). The machines you placed then are about to find out what they are made of.

Meeting that demand on your own dime means loading heavier and loading more often. Heavier loads put more of your money in the machines at the same moment your other bills climb. More frequent loads mean more trips, more scheduling, and more of your Saturday spent on logistics instead of the business. And a terminal that runs dry on a holiday weekend loses the transaction, the surcharge, and a little of the location's faith in you.

We have said before that [holiday uptime gets decided weeks ahead](/resources/blog/nobody-takes-labor-day-off-your-atms-least-of-all). Cash supply is half of that equation. A machine with a perfect connection and an empty cassette is still a very expensive sign that says "Sorry."

So the operator heading into December faces a choice that does not feel like much of a choice: tie up more capital, or gamble on empty machines when they matter most.
