Labor Day is the one holiday that celebrates work by not doing any. That is the whole joke of it. The country agrees, more or less all at once, to stop.

Except the country does not stop. It relocates.

It goes to the lake. The ballpark. The state fair. The campground three hours out where the signal gets thin and the taproom has a handwritten sign that says CASH ONLY and means it. The economy does not slow down over Labor Day weekend. It picks up and moves somewhere with worse infrastructure.

Your ATMs go with it. Not physically, but in every way that matters to your revenue. The machine at the marina does more volume in those three days than it does in the three weeks on either side. The one at the fairgrounds wakes up from a ten-month nap. The convenience store off the interstate exit, the one you almost pulled last spring, suddenly looks like the smartest placement in the portfolio.

That is the good half.

## The Other Half

Here is the part nobody puts in the seasonal forecast. The weekend that produces your highest cash demand is also the weekend with your thinnest coverage.

Armored carriers run holiday schedules. Vault cash ordered Thursday afternoon has to carry the machine until Tuesday. Your service tech is at a cookout in another county. The location manager who would normally call you about the receipt paper is a nineteen-year-old running the register alone, and she is not going to call anybody about anything.

So a terminal that goes down at nine on Saturday morning is not down for an hour. It is down for the weekend. And at a seasonal location, the weekend is the season.

That is the real cost, and it never shows up as a line item. It shows up as a number that is lower than it should have been, in a month you have already stopped thinking about.

## Uptime Is Not a Purchase

Here is the uncomfortable version. You do not buy uptime over a holiday weekend. You either engineered it in August or you did not.

By Friday afternoon the variables are locked. The cash is in the machine or it is not. The connection is solid or it is the gas station's guest Wi-Fi with a captive portal that drops every ninety minutes. The terminal is running a supported configuration or it is running something a previous operator set up in 2019 and nobody has touched since.

Every one of those is a decision made weeks earlier by somebody who was not thinking about Labor Day.

This is why the operators who never sweat holiday weekends are not luckier than you. They are earlier than you. They treat the third week of August the way a restaurant treats prep. Nothing happens during service that was not decided during prep.

## What Monitoring Is Actually For

There is a version of ATM monitoring that is a dashboard. It logs the fault, colors the row red, and waits for someone to look at it.

On a Tuesday, that is fine. Somebody looks.

On Labor Day Sunday, nobody looks. That dashboard is a diary. It will tell you, accurately and in detail, exactly how much money you did not make.

The version that matters is the one where the alert has a human attached to it. Where a connection failure at the lake location gets caught, diagnosed, and in most cases resolved before the first family pulls in for ice and bait. Where somebody who is not you is awake and holding the pager.

Round-the-clock monitoring is not really about the alert. It is about who is on the other end of it. A machine that cannot tell anyone it is in trouble stays in trouble for as long as the holiday lasts.

## Here's the Move

<MoveLead>
Take twenty minutes this week, while the weekend is still fresh, and do the least glamorous exercise in this business.
</MoveLead>

Pull your terminal-level numbers for Saturday, Sunday and Monday. Not the portfolio total. Individual machines. Then find the ones that went quiet. Not the ones that ran low, the ones that stopped. Some of them stopped because the location closed. Some of them stopped because something broke and no one was there to notice.

Those two look identical on a monthly report. They are not remotely the same problem.

Then look at the ones that ran dry. That is a forecasting question, not a fault, and it has a cleaner fix: better vault cash planning, or a recycler at the locations where your own deposits can do the work your armored run is currently doing.

Do this now, in September, and you have six weeks to fix what you find. Do it in December and you are reading a postmortem on Q4.

The next three-day weekend is Columbus Day, October 12. Then Thanksgiving, which is bigger. Then the stretch from Black Friday to New Year's Day, which is not a weekend at all but a five-week holiday your fleet has to survive on the configuration it has right now.

Everybody working today deserved the day off. Your machines did not get one, and they are not going to get one in November either.
