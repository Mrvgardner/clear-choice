A customer walks up to your counter in the second week of December holding a gift bag and a problem. The holiday market across the street is cash only. They ask whether you have an ATM.

Your cashier points down the block. "There's one at the gas station on the corner."

The customer says thanks and walks out. Maybe they come back. Maybe they buy their coffee, their gift card and their last-minute stocking stuffer at the gas station on the corner instead, because they are already standing there and the line is shorter.

You just sent a customer to a competitor, and you did it politely.

## The Walkout Nobody Counts

Most store owners never measure this, because there is nothing to measure. A sale that never happened does not show up on the register tape. Nobody logs "asked for an ATM, left." The customer simply is not there anymore, and the day closes a little lighter than it should have.

But it happens more than most owners think, and December makes it worse. Holiday shoppers carry more cash, need more cash and run into more places that only take cash: the market vendor, the valet, the tip jar, the kid selling wreaths for the school fundraiser. People still reach for cash at the moments that matter, a point we made back in May when we wrote that [cash is not dead, it just moved](/resources/blog/cash-is-not-dead-it-just-moved). When your customer needs it, they will go get it. The only question is whose door they walk through to do it.

Run a quick, honest example. Say three customers a day ask for an ATM and leave to find one. Over the thirty-one days of December, that is more than ninety people walking out of your store at the exact moment they had money on their mind. Even if most of them come back, the ones who do not are the ones who found a store they like better.

And a customer who leaves to find cash is not just one lost sale. They are a customer who has learned that your store is not the one to stop at when they need cash. That lesson sticks well past the holidays.

## What an ATM Actually Does for a Store

An ATM inside your store does three things, and only one of them is obvious.

The obvious one: it keeps the customer in the building. They get cash, and the shortest path to spending it is your counter. A customer who never leaves is a customer who never gets distracted by somebody else's shelf.

The second one is less obvious. It changes what kind of stop your store is. A store with an ATM becomes a destination for people who need cash, which in December is a lot of people. Some of them came in only for the machine. Many of them leave with something else in hand.

The third one is the one owners like best once they see it. Clear Choice gives store owners a choice in how the machine is funded. We can manage the cash for you with vaulting services, or you can fill the machine with your own sales proceeds. That second option turns the cash piling up in your register into cash your customers withdraw, which means less of it sitting in the back office and fewer trips to the bank. We wrote last month about [what the holiday rush does to a register](/resources/blog/black-friday-breaks-twenties). An ATM is one more way to keep that cash moving.
