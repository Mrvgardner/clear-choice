Compliance isn't new to the ATM industry — but enforcement is changing.

Visa has recently updated its expectations around Electronic Journaling (EJ), reinforcing how critical accurate, accessible transaction records are when disputes, investigations, or audits occur. For operators, this isn't a theoretical shift. It has real consequences — including the risk of fines, escalations, or even terminal shutdowns if records can't be produced properly.

The good news:
Switch Commerce and Clear Choice partners are already equipped to meet these requirements.

---

## What's Changing with Visa and Electronic Journaling

Visa's updated guidance places increased responsibility on acquirers and operators to ensure that complete, accurate electronic journal records are available when requested.

### That means:

- EJ records must be captured consistently
- Records must be retrievable within required timeframes
- Data must be accurate and clearly tied to each terminal
- Gaps, missing logs, or incomplete data can no longer be brushed aside

This is no longer a "best practice."
It's a requirement.

---

## Why Electronic Journaling Matters More Than Ever

Electronic Journals play a critical role in:

- Resolving transaction disputes
- Supporting chargeback responses
- Demonstrating compliance during reviews
- Protecting operators from liability

Without reliable EJ data, operators are exposed — even if everything else in their operation is solid.

Visa's updated stance reinforces something the industry has known for a while: documentation is protection.
