Every October, the convenience industry picks one city and empties into it. This year the city is Las Vegas, the dates are October 6 through 9, and we will be on the floor with machines you can actually put your hands on.

The NACS Show is four days at the Las Vegas Convention Center where the entire c-store world, retailers, suppliers, tech vendors, and everyone selling something to all three, shows up at once. For an operator, it is the one week of the year when every vendor you deal with is standing in the same building, obligated to answer questions face to face. That is worth a plane ticket all by itself.

Here is our honest opinion about trade shows: most booths are a brochure with better lighting. A rendering of a machine tells you what the design team wanted. It tells you nothing about how the hardware feels when a customer is standing in front of it at 6 a.m. trying to get cash before a shift. You cannot evaluate payment equipment from a spec sheet, and you should never sign a processing relationship because somebody had impressive carpet.

So we are bringing the real thing. Actual hardware on the floor, including the Genmega UK2 kiosk. Walk up to it. Put your hands on it. Judge the build quality up close, because that is the honest way to judge it, and it is exactly the evaluation a rendering is designed to help you skip.

Why does a payments company care this much about a c-store show? Because convenience stores sit at the center of what we do. The payment stack in a c-store is not an accessory; it is infrastructure: the ATM that drives foot traffic and surcharge revenue, the processing that has to clear every card without drama, the monitoring that notices a problem before the morning rush does. Decisions about that stack tend to get made in October, in person, at this show. We intend to be part of those conversations.

## Here's the Move

<MoveLead>
Build your vendor list before you fly, and put the same four questions to every payments booth on it. What is your actual uptime, a number, not an adjective. When does my money settle, in days, weekends included. When a terminal goes down at 2 a.m., who notices first, you or me? And what does a dispute cost me in hours? A booth that answers in plain terms is showing you what their support line sounds like. A booth that answers in paragraphs is showing you that too.
</MoveLead>

One practical tip from people who have walked a lot of show floors: do your mapping in the exhibitor directory before you land, not in the aisles after. Four days sounds like plenty until you are standing in a hall the size of an airport deciding between hundreds of booths and a coffee line. The operators who get real value out of NACS arrive with a short list of vendors, a shorter list of questions, and appointments already on the calendar. The ones who wing it get a tote bag.

The show floor gets loud and calendars fill fast, so if you want real time with the team instead of a handshake in a crowd, set it up before you fly.
